# Standup

Every morning, ten minutes, standing. Not a status report to a manager —
a synchronisation between people who will otherwise build the same thing
twice.

## The format

Three sentences each, in this order:

1. **Finished yesterday** — an issue number. "US-102" tells the team
   something; "I worked on the search" does not.
2. **Taking today** — one issue. If it is the same as yesterday, say
   why.
3. **Blocked by** — a person, a decision or a missing thing, named.

## The two rules that make it work

**A blocker is assigned before the standup ends.** Not "we'll look into
it". A name and a time.

**Anything longer than a sentence leaves the standup.** Two people carry
it on afterwards, alone. The others go back to work.

## The three questions the delivery hat asks

Silently, while listening:

- Is anything In Progress for more than two days? That is a blocker
  nobody has said out loud.
- Is anyone holding two stories at once? One per person, always.
- Does the current batch still fit before the milestone?

## If the team is remote that day

Same three sentences, written in the team channel before the usual time,
and a ten-minute call anyway if anybody is blocked. A written standup
with no call works exactly as long as nobody is stuck.

## What is not in the standup

Design debates, architecture decisions, scope changes. They have their
own moments — see [`../CEREMONIES.md`](../CEREMONIES.md).
